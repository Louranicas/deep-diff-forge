# DDF-2026-008: a judge that left a process behind could stretch an agent's stop past its limit

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Severity | Not rated by an assessment. A deadline breach: a stop states that it is done within 45 s (`STOP_LIMIT`; a registered `Stop` hook gets 60 s from `agent setup --hooks`), and it was not. No verdict was changed: a stop that ran long ended UNJUDGED or was ended by the client with its answer dropped, never passed |
| Affected | 0.9.0, the first release with agent hooks. Measured on 2026-11-23 on a build of the 0.9.0 code (commit `db9df06d`, whose tree is the tag `v0.9.0`'s; the tag names commit `7fb73f13`; binary `5ba0a366…`, not the published 0.9.0 binary `83158afe…`): 75.1 s for a stop whose judge exited at once. 0.8.3 and 0.8.4 (measured on the published 0.8.3 binary `4cafd0ad…` and the 0.8.4 release binary `0c93b3ed…`) have no `agent hook` command (exit 2). The same wait is in the council's Git and `tuicr` calls of 0.8.3 to 0.9.0; through those it was not measured |
| Fixed in | 0.10.0. What stays open is under "What 0.10.0 does not change" |
| Found | 2026-11-22, by the design critic of the 0.10.0 task plan, with a stand-in judge |

## What went wrong

Every child DDF waits for runs through one bounded runner (`run_bounded` in
`crates/deep-diff-forge-core/src/process.rs`): a time limit, an output cap, and a kill when
either is passed. The readers of the child's output were detached when the child was killed,
but after a child that exited by itself they were joined with no deadline. A process the child
had started and left running kept the output pipe open, and the join waited for it. The stop
hook runs the judge (forge-warden) through this runner, so a judge that exited at once while a
grandchild of it held its output made the stop wait for that grandchild, however long it ran.

## What this could cause

A stop that runs past the time its client gives it is ended by the client, and its answer is
dropped: the session ends with no word from DDF on the change. A stop that ends later than its
limit but before the client's also leaves the rest of its work (listing the session's commits,
judging them) no time, so it ends UNJUDGED. Measured on 2026-11-23 with a stand-in judge that
starts `sleep 75` in the background and exits 0 (`agent hook stop --client claude --judge …`
on a repository with one uncommitted change; "the fix" is the 0.10.0 candidate, commit
`d08ade1d`):

| | 0.8.3 | 0.8.4 | 0.9.0 | the fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The stop's time | no hooks (exit 2) | no hooks (exit 2) | 75.1 s | 0.15 s |
| The judge's grandchild after the stop | | | ran to its end | killed |

## How to check a version

`deep-diff-forge --version` prints the version; `agent hook` exists from 0.9.0. The test
`crates/deep-diff-forge-cli/tests/judge_deadline.rs` runs the stand-in judge above against the
binary it builds and fails when the stop takes 45 s or more, or when the grandchild outlives it.

## What 0.10.0 changes

- Each child of the runner is the leader of a process group of its own, and the whole group is
  killed on every way out: before the reap when the time limit or the output cap is passed, and
  right after the reap when the child exits by itself. What the judge leaves running in its
  group is killed with it.
- The output readers and the input writer are joined only while the deadline lasts, and are
  detached after it, so the call returns by its time limit plus about one poll interval and the
  time to kill and reap, whatever holds the pipes. A child that exited in time whose pipes are
  still held at the deadline is reported as past its time limit.
- The group kill is `killpg` through the `nix` crate (0.31.3, already used by the council's
  sandbox for the same pattern); no `unsafe` code and no new crate in the lockfile.

## What 0.10.0 does not change

- A process that leaves the group (it makes its own session with `setsid`, or detaches by a
  double fork into a group of its own) is not killed by DDF. The call still returns by its
  deadline, but that process runs on. Under Claude Code 2.1.330 the client ends a timed-out
  hook's descendants, including those in their own group or session (measured 2026-11-22); a
  process that detaches from them is ended by no one.
- Because the child is no longer in DDF's own process group, a signal from the terminal
  (Ctrl-C) reaches DDF and not the child; a DDF ended by it leaves the child to finish on its
  own.
- Git acquisition (`git_acquire.rs`, which builds the judge's patch and reads a change) has its
  own runner; its bounded join is a separate fix of the same release (S3.17a.b).

## What to do

1. Use 0.10.0 from the signed tag `v0.10.0`: its release archive checked against the signed
   `SHA256SUMS` (`scripts/release_local.sh verify`), or a build of the tag.
2. With 0.9.0, a stop that ends with no message, or UNJUDGED with "did not finish in the time
   a hook has", after a judge ran is the sign. Only a judge that leaves a process running
   with its output open causes it.
